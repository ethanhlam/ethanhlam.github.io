Project Aegis — Underactuated Robotic Hand

A 38-part, 4-finger robotic hand (Hand 3.0) driven by a single hand-cranked gear axle, engineered and scaled from an open reference design into a fully custom assembly.

Role
Sole Designer / Fabricator
Timeline
2026
Tools
SOLIDWORKS, FDM, Laser Cut
Part Count
38 Components

Overview

Project Aegis is an underactuated, 4-finger robotic hand built around a single manual drive crank. Turning the crank rotates a central axle fitted with four laser-cut plywood half-gears, each of which meshes with a gear-toothed "Mid Rotator" in its corresponding finger — curling all four fingers simultaneously from one input motion. The design was scaled roughly 3.22x from a reference finger geometry ("Phalanges") and rebuilt into a complete hand assembly.

Design & Engineering Process

  • Modeled all 32 custom 3D-printed components in SOLIDWORKS, including 7 unique parts per finger module (tip, side linkages, mid connectors, gear-toothed rotator, and mount).
  • Engineered the palm chassis with integrated slide-in slots for tool-free finger assembly and a recessed window for a clear acrylic cover plate.
  • Designed the drive train: a single 0.25" wooden dowel axle carrying four laser-cut plywood half-gears, retained by 3D-printed spacing washers to prevent lateral slip.
  • Diagnosed and resolved a locked-joint failure traced to a degenerate geometry fragment in the STL export — resolved by checking fork slot clearance and re-exporting at finer mesh resolution.

Manufacturing

32 of the hand's 38 components were FDM 3D printed in PLA. The remaining 6 — four gear segments and the acrylic cover plate — were laser cut from 0.25" plywood and 0.125" clear acrylic, chosen for the rigidity needed at the drive interface and to keep the internal mechanism visible.

Outcome

The finished assembly demonstrates a working underactuated gear-drive mechanism — all four fingers curl in sync from a single manual input, with the acrylic cover plate showing the gear train in motion.
